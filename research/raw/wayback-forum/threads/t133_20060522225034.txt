The Perseid meteor shower peaks the night of Thursday 11 August 2005 into the morning of Friday 12 August 2005.
The best viewing time is just after midnight Thursday night (when the moon has set).
>> Quote: 
 [quote] Earth is now travelling through debris left behind by a comet, called Comet Swift-Tuttle. This comet visits our region of the solar system about once every 130 years. Swift-Tuttle last came near the sun in December, 1992. Meteor showers are often richest when their parent comets are nearby, so, since the 1990s, the Perseid meteor shower has been reliably spectacular each year that the moon was out of the way. Luckily, the moon, which is nearly first quarter tonight, will have set before midnight and will thus not be a hindrance to tonight's display. Today's chart shows the constellation Perseus on very early Friday morning. This is the radiant point of the Perseid shower. It's ZHR, or Zenithal Hourly Rate -- the number of meteors seen at the shower's peak, under optimum conditions -- is close to 90. In other words, at the shower's peak, in an hour, you might expect to see 90 meteors. In reality, the rate of viewable meteors varies from time to time and location. Predictions for meteor showers have improved in recent years, but these showers are still a gamble. The peak of this year's Perseid meteor shower is predicted for tonight. After midnight (Friday morning) is the best time to look. The bits of ice and dust left behind by Swift-Tuttle will be striking our atmosphere at speeds of up to 40 miles a second. The cometary debris will vaporize due to friction with the air, and we'll see each vaporizing bit as a bright streak in our night sky: a shooting star, or meteor. This is often the richest shower of the year as seen from the northern hemisphere. The best way to watch the shower is simply to find a dark location, away from city lights. Forget about the location of the radiant point of the shower -- just relax, and let your eyes roam over the entire sky. While you're up, you can also find the planet Mars in the east after midnight.
