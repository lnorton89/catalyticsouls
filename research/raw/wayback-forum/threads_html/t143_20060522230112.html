OK I'll touch briefly on this genre thing but i think you should start a topic n general discussion to get really into it.

OK just to go with two extremes of style and technique I will use house and hip-hop for comparison on DJ technique and to begin teh understanding of the importance of classification. (keep in mind this evolved with technics wiht an +-8% so some mixing can now be done wiht extreme pitchshift with key correction that weren't historically possible and will allow the dj to cross some genre lines)

OK hip hop well lets say rap: When hearing rap at clubs mixed live what you are usually going to hear is some scratches and then a cut.. possibly some more advnced techniques involving beat jugling... but often times you will not here extended blending through beat matching for a few reasons. 1 the tempos could range from ~125 to ~80 which is beyond the correction capable on stadard tables. Secondly while there are a few basic rythmic patterns often found in rap it is increasingly diverse in the beats it is built on and subject to non- 4/4 time signatures and pitch variations. So to mix something so varied you come up with interesting scratch techniques and maybe some short teases and other methods to transfer from track to track while keeping the floor moving.
Now house in comparison is often mixed with the goal of seemlessly overlaying one or more tracks sometimes playing several minutes all simulatneously forming new musical creations in the process. Now to make this possible the drums need to be of the same time signature and roughly the same tempo (at least in range of the pitch adjustment) and have a similar pattern to the drums so that they do not sound cluttered when playing simultaneously. (DJ DAN and CARL COX are excellent djs to check out for these styles) but sometimes techniques converge... some hiphop djs beatmatch and sbly blend in much the same style as a house dj would and some house djs scratch and cut and make quick mixes much like a hip -hop dj (think bad boy bill) House is often in the 120s BPM. Then ther eis jungle which has reggae influenced basslines running around 80BPM with doubletimed drums on the top running ~160 BPM or so. Well you can see right away that with your 160bpm you will not be able to match the tempo with house tracks on your standard turntables.. But interestingly you may be able to overlay Hip-hop at half time to this (Look up Simply Butta (Riot Squad) for some killer examples of this hip-hop styling taken to Jungle.. Also a little closer to home is CM3 an reperkushin who do similar things with reggae and jungle. So your genres and sub genres are usually telling you a beat structure and general tempo to expect and then they are divided further and furtehr which is helpful fo rdj's record shopping to know that their taste and styles tend to fall in to a certain specific category and instead of sifting through the 1000's of independent and label releases each month for the tracks with proper tempos and patterns to flow with what they are doing they can examine only those that have the potential to blend wiht their work.
Then there's breaks which have shared roots with hip-hop with a tempo nearer to house. You will find a number of dj's, especially in the midwest, blending house and breaks (of those in the area Andy B and Tony Foo Yong wouold be good examples)

There are many more styles and variations out there.. so as i said befoer fell free to throw this in general discussion to find out more styles and what makes them different
