ELECTRIC SKYCHURCH
http://www.skychurch.com
James Lumb created Electric Skychurch as a vehicle for his imaginative, groundbreaking music in 1991. In '93 Skychurch rose up in the LA scene. James was throwing parties at his home in Silverlake, a LA neighborhood teeming with musicians, filmmakers and artists. The late-night events took off, eventually moving from LA to the Mojave Desert, where the parties grew into large monthly gatherings. Within a year, the gatherings exploded to include thousands of people and seeded the notorious Desert Massive movement that swept through the American West. During this period Lumb penned "Deus", a song that would come to be recognized as the earliest incarnation of the "West Coast" trance sound. Burgeoning Moonshine Music signed Skychurch in 1994 and released "Deus" and another single, "Creation", followed in 1995 by Skychurch's first full-length ensemble LP, "knowoneness."

During the knowoneness sessions, James met Roxanne Morganstern. They were friends for a year before she finally auditioned for a spot in the band. Impressed with her unique and powerful talent, James immediately brought her into the project and, even though it shocked his band mates, shared the spotlight with her the very next week in front of 5,000 fans. The vocal chemistry worked. Fans loved it. They created a magical live show, which instantly catapulted them even further into the spotlight.

After playing hundreds of concerts and festivals that drew crowds in excess of 80,000, Electric Skychurch built a solid grassroots following. During the long tour, James and Roxanne were filmed by director John Reese for interviews that became part of the feature documentary "Better Living Through Circuitry," giving the world a refreshingly honest portrait of the producer and his band mates. The film remains popular and continues to run on the major cable networks worldwide, and has drawn mainstream fans of all ages to the band.
