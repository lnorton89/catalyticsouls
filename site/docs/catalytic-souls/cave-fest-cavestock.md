# Cave Fest and CaveStock (2004–2011)

The jam-band-plus-DJ-stage camping weekends that funded Underground Sound. Cave Fest was the spring show (later branded "The Original 420 Show" / "Earth Day Gathering"); CaveStock was the fall show ("Shawnee Harvest Fest"). Both were partnerships between Tom Hughes and cave owner Bob Goodale, whose relationship the thesis describes as on-again, off-again: Goodale co-financed CaveStock 1 and Cave Fest 2006, refused to partner on CaveStock 2, and "insisted on partnering again" for Cave Fest 2006. The crew's website doubled as the cave's calendar ("your web connection to Caveman Bob and Gettin Lucky Productions"), which is why it also lists Gettin' Lucky's own Blues Fest and Cave Rock Festival in 2005.

## What the thesis adds

The Cave Fest / CaveStock chapters are the most practical pages in the thesis: the back-end-percentage argument (why a ten-band festival can't pay 30% door deals), the coupon test that revealed The Nightlife magazine as the only advertising that worked, the flyer-printing failures, the discovery that a Friday post-sunset slot at a camping event beats a 2 a.m. "prime" slot, the acoustics work to keep stages from bleeding, and the decision to hand satellite stages to regional crews. Read them in the [full text](/thesis/full-text#cavestock-1-my-first-lesson-from-promotions-101).

## Why they mattered

Cave Fest and CaveStock were the cash flow and the goodwill. They drew the SIU and jam-band crowd, kept Goodale's venue busy in shoulder seasons, and, in the author's telling, paid off Disco Donnie after UGS 1 and funded the regional club circuit that built the TomFoolery name. When Catalytic Souls left after 2008, Goodale kept the format alive for three more seasons before the gate closed.
