# The CatalyticSouls label and the TomFoolery discography

## The label (2007–2010)

The thesis describes setting up CatalyticSouls as "a digital imprint for multiple genres of dance music," distributed by Symphonic Distribution and approved for Beatport "even though Beatport.com is officially not taking on any new labels." The plan: zero upfront cost, gather quarterly sales data by artist and style, then press vinyl through a European distributor as a booking tool.

What Discogs preserves (label id 134243): fourteen digital releases, all by TomFoolery, several with DJ Fixx, catalog numbers CS001 to CS108, from September 30, 2007 to 2010. No vinyl. No other artists.

Observations:

- The first release shipped the month before the thesis interviews, so "I have recently set up one of those myself" was literally true when written.
- "Defiance (Transformer Man Mix)" is the commissioned UK remix the thesis discusses as a way to attach a known name to his production.
- The catalog-number gaps (001, 007, 009, 012...) suggest planned releases that never shipped, or numbering shared with the distributor's system.
- The multi-artist, multi-genre, vinyl-graduating label the thesis envisioned never materialized. It stayed a one-artist digital outlet and went quiet in 2010, the year of the last Camp Zoe festival.

## Mix CDs (pre-label)

From a 2005 crew bio and the thesis: *Progression of Breaks* (2001), *TopCat presents TomFoolery* (2002), *NuSkool Compilation* (2003), *Fool in da Breaks* (2004), *Resolution* (2005), *USAmnesia* (2006; 1,000 professionally pressed copies given away as promotion, mastered for car listening, artwork of a turntable needle in homage to the Moonshine mix-CD era). None are on Discogs; if you have one, it may be the only documented copy.

## dubTomFoolery (2018)

A Bandcamp release, *listens back* (February 2018, Orlando): "hardware sequenced, generated and dub mixed in real time through miles of cable, tons of circuits, & a ghost 32 LE." The author of a thesis about the laptop's conquest of the DJ booth closed his recorded output on an all-hardware dub rig. His Facebook page's final post (February 27, 2021): "Still love using tapes."
