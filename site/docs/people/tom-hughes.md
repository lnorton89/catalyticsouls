# Tom Hughes (TomFoolery), 1978–2022

::: warning
Tom Hughes appears to have died in early 2022, aged 43. The only source found is a February 5, 2022 Facebook post by his Bay Area Records collaborator Lo IQ?: "A sweet and kind soul has passed Tom Hughes aka Tom Foolery i will miss you." No obituary surfaced by web search. The date is consistent with his Florida LLC's last annual report (January 2021) and its dissolution for non-filing (September 2022), and with his Facebook page's last post (February 27, 2021). Treat the date as approximate until an obituary is found.
:::

## Who he was, in his own framing

The thesis is unusually candid about motive. He wanted to build "a multi-sensory audio-visual spectacular." He was a lighting person before he was a DJ ("The aspect I found lacking in most events was lighting. This is where I wanted to start"). He believed in the DJ as conduit rather than star, and distrusted the "unhealthy ego-trip" of DJ worship even while working hard to get his own name in lasers. He wrote openly about losing money, about being lied about online, about a friend leaking his plans, and about taking any gig "regardless of timeslot or pay (usually a lack there of)." He stopped drinking at a rave and stayed stopped. He was an economics graduate who framed the rave as a public good that "could be used for economically productive means" and chose it anyway.

A 2005 crew bio puts it in flyer language: "one of the most prominent breaks DJs in the central United States... Influenced by the hard/funky south and the deep/progressive mid-west," following "Paul van Dyk, Carl Cox, and Uberzone where the DJ was more than a musician but a spiritual guide."

## The arc, seen whole

- He couldn't afford Full Sail in 1999 and became a course director there two decades later.
- He wrote a thesis about the laptop's conquest of the DJ booth and ended his recorded output on an all-hardware dub rig, posting about tape.
- He wrote that he'd never netted a profit on an electronic event and kept throwing them for four more years across three states.
- He started as a breaks partisan in a house-and-techno Midwest where his genre was "almost taboo," booked psytrance headliners nobody else in the region would, and by 2011 described himself as a bass-music DJ, which is where the whole Midwest camping-festival scene went.
- The RAVE Act he attacked was never used against anyone, but its parent statute took the second home of his festival.
