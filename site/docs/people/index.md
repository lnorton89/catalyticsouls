# Everyone else in the story

For the author, see [Tom Hughes / TomFoolery](/people/tom-hughes). The crew's own DJs are on [The crew](/catalytic-souls/).

## Southern Illinois

**Robert "Caveman Bob" Goodale and Lou Goodale.** Owners and operators of the Shawnee Salt Petre Cave from about 2000 to its closure. Bob was an SIU student in 1976–77 who helped organize concerts at the cave in 1978; roots in Centralia; ice cream, coal and firewood businesses; reportedly bought the cave cheaply after saving the previous owner's life. Kept a notebook of every band's rate. Co-financed some Catalytic Souls shows and not others. Called "Santa" for his beard; a 2008 writer described him in shorts and an unbuttoned Hawaiian shirt. Ran diminishing seasons alone after 2008; his site pleaded for volunteers from 2008; the gate closed with a for-sale sign by August 2012. The Southern Illinoisan called him "the last face of the cave." No obituary or current information found. Lou "fixes up some of her famous BBQ" at Cave Fest, per the 2005 Southern preview.

**Shane Wade and Jeff Parrish.** Parrish acquired the cave in 2012 or 2013 and let it sit; Wade (grew up in Herrin, Carterville High, lives in Marion) manages and co-owns it. They rebuilt through 2020 and reopened October 31, 2020 as the Shawnee Cave Amphitheater, programming jamgrass, blues and Americana at a self-imposed ~3,000 cap. Wade's 2023 Daily Yonder interview is the only on-record account of why the rave era ended. The venue cancelled its August 2024 festival and has shown no events since.

**Gib Todd.** Murphysboro; built the cave venue in the late 1960s and ran national acts there through the 1970s and 80s.

**Phylis Johnson.** Hughes' major professor. Joined SIU's radio-television faculty in 1990, chaired the department 2002–05 and again on an interim basis in 2008. Now SIU Emeritus Professor of Sound and New Media, Professor at San José State, founding co-editor of *Resonance: The Journal of Sound and Culture* (UC Press). **Todd Herreman** and **Jay Needham** (SIU faculty) are thanked in the thesis; **Mike Lescelius** let Hughes sit in on sessions.

**Broken Grass, the Rum Runners, Defined Perception, Jake's Leg, Helping Phriendly, Cornmeal.** The regional jam, ska and tribute acts that filled Cave Fest and CaveStock bills. Broken Grass was "at their peak of popularity and believed to be on the verge of major national success" in 2004; the thesis credits their following with saving CaveStock 1. Defined Perception made its sixth cave appearance in 2007.

**WDBX 91.1 FM** (Carbondale community radio) lent Hughes the classic-rock vinyl for his first cave gig; hearing a WDBX DJ play breaks was his first connection to the local scene. **The Copper Dragon** (Carbondale club) hosted the live multitrack session that introduced him to the area's top bands. **The Nightlife** (local entertainment weekly) turned out to be the only advertising that moved tickets. **Plaza Records** and **Old Towne Liquors** in Carbondale sold Cave Fest tickets.

## The regional electronic scene

**Woody McBride (DJ ESP).** Minneapolis techno producer and promoter (Communiqué Records, +8 affiliations, "The Genius of Fun"), "outspoken straight edge raver," backer of the 2004 Caveman Experience. In the thesis he is the digital pessimist ("catastrophic... Albert Einstein and his release of the atom bomb") and the man who warned Disco Donnie off Hughes in 2005. Whatever that was, it healed: he played Cave Fest '07 and Underground Sound 5 and 6. Since: relaunched Communiqué on vinyl and digital (reissues through 2023), still touring, played the 25th-anniversary Even Furthur in Wisconsin.

**Disco Donnie Estopinal.** New Orleans promoter whose State Palace Theater raves were the target of the 2001 crack-house prosecution the thesis describes (charges dropped after the ACLU intervened; the venue owners paid a fine). Talent buyer and financial backstop for UGS 1 and 2. Sold Disco Donnie Presents to SFX in 2012 (its first acquisition), bought it back from LiveStyle on April 1, 2020 (the first EDM founder to do so), rebranded as Disco Presents for its 30th anniversary in 2024; roughly 700 shows a year; owns Ubbi Dubbi, Sunset Music Festival, Freaky Deaky and Lights All Night.

**Intergrüv Networks and Tim Fusion.** The Chicago crew that created the Caveman Experience in 2003 and expanded it in 2004 with Middleschool Productions and Woody McBride's Genius of Fun; Tim Fusion played the "main event" slot in 2003 and headlined the 2005 "Grave Dancing" night in Carbondale that Spacehead co-promoted. The thesis's unnamed "Chicago promoter" who campaigned against Hughes in 2005 is described as the person "who had taken most of the credit for the Caveman Series"; the recovered Intergrüv site credits the event to the crew as a whole, and this wiki does not identify the individual further. Intergrüv's 2004 site is the best surviving picture of the Midwest network Catalytic Souls inherited: Ghetto Puff, Toy Maker, Boogie Knights, Syde-Sho, Electric Soul Patrol, Free Agent and two dozen more crews from Fargo to Memphis.

**Eric Paxton Stauder (Dots per Minute).** Flyer designer for UGS 1 and 2, quoted in the *Time Europe* rave piece the thesis cites.

**Andy B, Unkl Ryan (Ryan Montgomery), Brian Dervish (Tekfro), Reperkushin, DJ Derve, Dave Skeezy, DJ Brainstorm, Spacehead, DJ Fixx, Tommy I.C. Gunns, Nasty Nate, Deadboy, Redi Jedi, DJ Blizzy, King David.** The crew roster; see [The crew](/catalytic-souls/).

**Benji Ramsey.** VJ at UGS 3 through 6 and maker of the UGS 6 promo video; now a certified home inspector who is "also a touring VJ." **Electronic Assault** (Clarksville, Tennessee) and "SSI" ran Cavetronic at the cave in July 2009, the electronic event that followed Catalytic Souls' departure. **Spoololife**, the collective whose 2005 crew page carried a Tom Foolery bio, is a national rave crew with Perfecto and Ministry of Sound credits, not a Southern Illinois one.

**Gettin' Lucky Productions.** The other promoter working the cave in 2005–2006 (Blues Fest, The Cave Rock Festival, The Spirit of Ozz); shared the Catalytic Souls website as a calendar. **ToyMaker Music Promotions** (Bloomington IL) and the **Nepol** DJs (St. Louis) were the crews TomFoolery played with before Cave Fest existed. **Chad and Jesse Designs** built the 2005–2008 website; **Benji Ramsey** was the VJ at UGS 3 through 6.

## The interviewees

**Jonathan Stewart (FACTORe).** "The top producer for his style in America and the first American to gain recognition for nu-skool breaks production in the British market." In October 2007 he'd taken a full-time job and hadn't released in a year; sales had gone from 2,000 to 500 per release. He did not stay retired: releases on Black Hole, Stripped and Dissident; runs the 20K Freaks label; co-owns Essential Freaks and the Season 4 Festival in Atlanta.

**Jorge Brea.** Founder of Symphonic Distribution (Tampa, late 2006), the distributor that got CatalyticSouls onto Beatport; the thesis calls him "DJ Viro" (an alias no other source uses, so treat as unverified). The optimist of the three interviews ("digital costs nothing but time"). Symphonic is now a ~200-person company with about $51.5M revenue (2024), a $37M Series B (2022), offices on four continents, 500,000+ artists, roughly $1B paid out lifetime.

## The headliners

**Infected Mushroom** (UGS 2, 2006): still headlining at Coachella and Tomorrowland scale; released *IM30* on January 30, 2026. **Electric Skychurch** (UGS 2): James Lumb continued sporadically; no significant recent activity. **Hallucinogen / Shpongle** (UGS 4): Simon Posford's projects; Shpongle's live band played its final shows in 2019 but Posford keeps DJing. **Bassnectar** (UGS 5) stopped touring in 2020 amid allegations. **Excision** (UGS 6) went on to found Lost Lands (2017), the largest bass-music festival in the US. **Rabbit in the Moon** (UGS 6), the Florida live-visual act the thesis's laser-and-scrim aesthetic descends from, still performs occasionally.

## The industry figures the thesis names

**Richie Hawtin** (digital vanguard; Final Scratch, Traktor): still touring; Traktor became a niche product while Pioneer/AlphaTheta CDJs and USB sticks won the booth. **deadmau5** (Beatport curiosity in 2007): seven Grammy nominations, the Cube, Kx5, a $55M catalog sale in 2025, an album in 2026. **Robert Sillerman** (the SFX Hughes stagehanded for, then the SFX that rolled up EDM): IPO 2013, bankrupt 2016, died 2019. **Pasquale Rotella / Insomniac**: half-owned by Live Nation since 2013; EDC Las Vegas 525,000 over three days in 2025. **Moonshine Music** (whose mix CDs *USAmnesia* imitated): collapsed by 2007; co-founder Ricardo Vinas went on to Thrive.
